# Calypso Digital Studio Website Foundation Design

Date: 2026-07-29
Status: Approved discovery direction, pending written-spec review

## 1. Purpose

Calypso Digital Studio needs a portfolio-led business website that does more than describe services. It must establish credibility, demonstrate design and software-development range, help nontechnical business owners understand what they may need, and convert visitors into organized project inquiries.

The website will operate as three connected systems:

1. A portfolio showing real website and software work.
2. A plain-language service guide for prospective clients.
3. A guided multi-step project intake flow that collects enough information to prepare recommendations and quotes.

The primary audience is small businesses, independent professionals, creators, and growing brands. The studio is based in New York's Capital Region but works with clients anywhere.

## 2. Positioning

Primary positioning statement:

> Custom websites and practical digital tools built around your business.

Supporting positioning:

> Calypso Digital Studio designs distinctive websites and custom software for small businesses, independent professionals, and growing brands.

The website should lead with website design and development because that is the clearest entry point for the target audience. Custom software and business tools remain a strong differentiator rather than an equal-weight technical category in the opening message.

The site should use plain language and explain business outcomes before technical implementation details.

## 3. Brand Direction

### 3.1 Creative concept

Official visual direction:

> Warm Creative Workshop — an editorial, autumn-inspired design studio with precise technical execution.

The site should feel:

- Creative, not chaotic
- Professional, not corporate
- Warm, not overly soft
- Technical, but understandable
- Personal, but capable of serving serious businesses
- Distinctive without distracting from the portfolio

The autumn influence must remain year-round and indirect. It should come from rust, cream, sage, charcoal brown, organic shapes, and tactile detail rather than literal leaves, pumpkins, or seasonal graphics.

### 3.2 Color system

Primary colors:

- Warm canvas: `#F7F1E7`
- Clean surface: `#FFFDF8`
- Primary ink: `#25231F`
- Signature rust: `#B64F30`

Secondary colors:

- Deep periwinkle: `#62659A`
- Forest sage: `#5D6C57`
- Muted clay: `#E5B29E`
- Lavender mist: `#D8D6EA`

Approximate visual balance:

- 60% cream and white
- 20% charcoal
- 10% rust
- 5% periwinkle
- 5% sage, clay, and lavender combined

Clay and lavender should be used primarily as surfaces and decorative fields, not normal body-text colors. Text and interactive controls must meet WCAG contrast requirements.

### 3.3 Typography

Recommended pairing:

- Fraunces for major headings, project titles, selected quotations, and editorial emphasis
- Manrope for body copy, navigation, buttons, labels, forms, and technical details

Typography rules:

- Sentence case is the default.
- Small uppercase labels may be used sparingly.
- Body text must remain at least 16px on mobile and approximately 17px on desktop.
- Mobile headings must be tested to prevent excessive line wrapping.
- Wide letter spacing and constant all-caps styling are prohibited.

### 3.4 Shape and texture

The design system should combine:

- Editorial rectangular panels
- Controlled rounded corners
- Thin borders
- Offset image compositions
- Cropped color fields
- Small circular labels
- Subtle hand-drawn arrows or underlines
- Organic shapes inspired indirectly by Calypso's markings

Texture may include subtle paper grain, fine halftone fields, soft shadows, and imperfect line details. Heavy scrapbook effects, fake paper tears, and distressed overlays are out of scope.

### 3.5 Logo use

The supplied square logo is the master visual asset. It will be used prominently on the About page, footer, social imagery, and selected branded panels.

The final system requires derived variants:

1. Horizontal header lockup
2. Compact stacked mark
3. Simplified emblem for small placements
4. Favicon
5. Transparent-background version
6. Single-color version for dark surfaces

The supplied JPEG must not be used as the favicon or as a small header mark without simplification because its texture and fine detail will not remain legible.

## 4. Information Architecture

Primary navigation:

- Home
- Services
- Work
- About
- Process
- Start Your Project

The final navigation item should be visually emphasized as the main conversion action.

### 4.1 Home

The homepage must include:

1. Asymmetrical hero with the main positioning statement
2. Primary action: Start Your Project
3. Secondary action: View Selected Work
4. Real project-preview composition using LRL Photography, Rare Treats 518, Good Intentions, and a custom-software panel
5. Service overview in plain language
6. Selected portfolio work
7. Custom business tools differentiator
8. Condensed working process
9. Guided intake preview

The hero must use real project captures rather than generic stock images or fake AI interfaces.

### 4.2 Services

Service categories:

- Website Design & Development
- Website Redesigns
- E-commerce and Online Selling
- Custom Business Tools
- Ongoing Support

Each service section should explain:

- The business situation it addresses
- Outcomes it can support
- Typical pages or features
- Related portfolio evidence
- A route into the guided intake flow

The page must avoid implying that every listed feature is included in every project.

- The story behind the Calypso name
- One natural founder photograph when supplied
- One image of Calypso when supplied

The copy should use first person where the working relationship is directly with Evan. The site must not imply a larger team.

### 4.5 Process

The process will be presented as:

1. Project inquiry
2. Discovery conversation
3. Proposal and scope
4. Design direction
5. Development
6. Review and revisions
7. Launch
8. Continued support

The page should explain what the client provides, what Calypso Digital Studio provides, and what happens at each stage. It should reduce uncertainty for first-time website clients without reading like a contract.

### 4.6 Start Your Project

This is a signature product feature, not a generic contact form.

First step project choices:

- New website
- Website redesign
- Online store
- Custom digital tool
- Ongoing website support
- Not sure yet

The flow will conditionally display relevant questions.

Website-project path:

1. Contact and business information
2. Business goals
3. Desired pages
4. Desired features
5. Existing materials
6. Visual preferences
7. Budget, timeframe, support needs, and contact preferences
8. Review and submission

Custom-software path:

1. Business problem or process being improved
2. Intended users
3. Current workflow
4. Information that must be entered, stored, or displayed
5. Account or login needs
6. Payment requirements
7. External service integrations
8. Definition of success
9. Budget, timeframe, and contact preferences
10. Review and submission

The form must include:

- A visible progress indicator
- Conditional questions
- Plain-language descriptions
- A review screen before submission
- Client-side validation for immediate feedback
- Server-side validation for security and data integrity
- Spam protection
- Accessible labels and keyboard navigation
- Error recovery that preserves entered data
- A clear noncommitment statement
- A success screen explaining next steps
- A confirmation email or submission copy when the email system is configured

The noncommitment statement should communicate that submitting the form does not commit the visitor to purchasing services; it helps the studio understand the project and prepare recommendations.

## 5. Content Voice

The writing voice must be:

- Plainspoken
- Specific
- Confident
- Helpful
- Honest about recommendations and project status
- Free of unnecessary technical jargon

Preferred example:

> I build websites around what your customers need to understand and what your business needs to accomplish.

Prohibited patterns:

- Generic agency language
- Claims about a team that does not exist
- Invented client results
- Unsupported business history
- Internal development wording on customer-facing pages
- Repeated phrases such as "digital solutions," "foundation," or "architecture" where a normal customer-facing term is available

## 6. Technical Architecture

### 6.1 Stack

- Astro
- TypeScript
- Component-scoped or centralized CSS using a documented token system
- Node 22 for CI and builds
- Cloudflare Pages for preview and production deployment
- GitHub pull requests for all major work
- GitHub Actions for validation

The initial site should prefer server-rendered/static Astro output and minimal client-side JavaScript. Interactive islands should be limited to features that require them, especially navigation and the intake wizard.

### 6.2 Project structure

Recommended boundaries:

- `src/components/` for reusable interface components
- `src/layouts/` for site and case-study shells
- `src/pages/` for routes
- `src/content/` for portfolio projects and editable structured content
- `src/data/` for navigation, services, process steps, and form options
- `src/styles/` for tokens, global styles, typography, utilities, and component patterns
- `public/` for optimized static assets
- `functions/` or Astro server endpoints for intake submission when implemented
- `scripts/` for content and build-output validation

Content that may change frequently should not be buried inside large page components.

### 6.3 Intake data flow

Initial form flow:

1. User chooses project type.
2. The client displays relevant steps.
3. Answers are stored locally in form state.
4. Each step validates required fields before continuing.
5. The review screen presents a readable summary.
6. Submission is sent to a secure server-side endpoint.
7. The endpoint validates and normalizes the payload.
8. Spam checks are applied.
9. The formatted inquiry is delivered through a configured email provider or form service.
10. The server returns a success or structured error response.
11. The client shows confirmation or preserves data and displays recovery instructions.

Secrets must remain in Cloudflare environment variables and must never be committed to GitHub.

The foundation may include the form interface and a disabled or mock submission state before email credentials are configured, but the UI must clearly indicate that live submissions are not yet active in preview builds.

## 7. Asset Strategy

The repository currently contains `Archive 9.zip`, which includes source PDFs and the logo image.

During implementation:

- The ZIP will be unpacked outside the served site.
- Required assets will be copied into organized project folders.
- Large PDFs will not be shipped to normal visitors unless explicitly offered as downloads.
- Portfolio images will be converted into optimized web formats.
- Broken black or blank image regions from PDF exports will not be published.
- When necessary, live-site captures will replace unreliable PDF-rendered imagery.
- Image dimensions and alt text will be explicit.
- Responsive image variants will be generated where useful.

The raw source archive may remain in repository history, but the working tree should not depend on loading assets directly from the ZIP.

## 8. Responsive and Accessibility Requirements

Target review widths:

- 390px mobile
- 430px mobile
- Tablet layout
- 1440px desktop

Required checks:

- No horizontal overflow
- Navigation remains within viewport
- Touch targets are appropriately sized
- Forms are fully keyboard accessible
- Focus indicators are visible
- Color contrast meets WCAG AA
- Reduced-motion preference is respected
- Images have meaningful alternative text where applicable
- Decorative images are hidden from assistive technology
- Heading order remains logical
- Error messages are programmatically associated with fields

Mobile layouts must be designed intentionally rather than produced only by stacking desktop columns.

## 9. Motion

Allowed motion:

- Gentle portfolio reveals
- Small button lift or state change
- Animated link underlines
- Short transitions between intake steps
- Subtle project-image movement

Disallowed motion:

- Large parallax effects
- Cursor replacements
- Long loading animations
- Constant floating decoration
- Motion that delays navigation or reading

All nonessential animation must be removed or reduced when `prefers-reduced-motion` is enabled.

## 10. Validation and Testing

GitHub Actions should run:

- Locked dependency installation
- Content validation
- Astro checks
- TypeScript checks
- Production build
- Build-output verification

Additional implementation checks:

- Internal links resolve
- Required metadata is present
- Draft or incomplete projects are not indexed as finished work
- Preview-only actions remain disabled
- Portfolio data follows the schema
- Form option IDs remain stable
- Intake payload validation rejects unknown or malformed fields

Browser QA should cover:

- All routes at mobile and desktop widths
- Mobile navigation
- Portfolio links and case-study navigation
- Conditional form paths
- Back and next behavior
- Form-state preservation
- Validation messages
- Review summary
- Success and failure states
- Console errors and warnings

## 11. Initial Delivery Scope

The first implementation pull request should establish the foundation, not finish every production integration.

Included:

- Astro and TypeScript scaffold
- Package lock generated in CI or a compatible Node 22 environment
- Design tokens and global typography
- Shared header, navigation, footer, button, and layout primitives
- Structured content and data schemas
- Route shells for all six primary pages
- Logo and source-asset organization
- Initial homepage composition using real portfolio assets where reliable
- Baseline metadata, sitemap readiness, and accessibility structure
- Validation scripts and GitHub Actions workflow

Deferred to subsequent pull requests:

- Complete case-study page designs
- Final live-site recaptures
- Final logo derivatives
- Full guided intake interface
- Live email delivery
- Spam provider configuration
- Custom-software case study
- Final production domain and analytics

## 12. Success Criteria

The foundation is successful when:

- The site builds reproducibly with Node 22.
- The visual system clearly reflects the Warm Creative Workshop direction.
- Navigation and route architecture are stable.
- The homepage immediately communicates websites plus custom tools.
- Real portfolio work is presented without broken PDF imagery or misleading status.
- Content is structured for later editing.
- Mobile layouts contain no horizontal overflow.
- Accessibility basics are present from the start.
- CI validates content, types, and production output.
- The next pull requests can build case studies and the intake system without restructuring the foundation.
